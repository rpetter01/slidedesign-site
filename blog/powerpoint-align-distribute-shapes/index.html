How to Align and Distribute Shapes in PowerPoint

Published November 18, 2026 · 5 min read

Uneven spacing is the fastest way to make a slide look amateur. PowerPoint can fix it in two clicks, but only if you use Align and Distribute in the right order. Here is how.

Step 1: select the shapes

Click the first shape, then hold Shift and click the others. You can also drag a selection box around them, or press Ctrl + A to select everything on the slide.

Step 2: choose what to align to

Open Home > Arrange > Align. The same menu is on the Shape Format tab. At the bottom of the list there are two modes, and one of them is ticked:

  • Align Selected Objects: the shapes line up with each other.
  • Align to Slide: the shapes line up with the edges or the centre of the slide.

Most of the time you want Align Selected Objects. If your shapes jump to the edge of the slide, this setting is the reason.

Step 3: align

CommandWhat it doesGood for
Align Left / Center / Rightlines up the shapes on a vertical linea column of boxes
Align Top / Middle / Bottomlines up the shapes on a horizontal linea row of cards or icons

For a row of cards, use Align Middle. For a column of boxes, use Align Left or Align Center.

Step 4: distribute

With three or more shapes selected, choose Distribute Horizontally for a row, or Distribute Vertically for a column. PowerPoint keeps the first and the last shape where they are and spaces the ones in between so that the gaps are equal.

Distribute equalises the gaps, not the centres. If your shapes have different widths, the gaps will match, and that is what the eye reads as even.

The order that always works

  1. Move the first and the last shape to where you want the row to start and end.
  2. Select all the shapes in the row.
  3. Click Align Middle.
  4. Click Distribute Horizontally.

Make them the same size first

A tidy row of shapes with different sizes still looks messy. Select all of them and open the Format Shape pane. Type one value in Height and one in Width, and every selected shape gets exactly that size.

A faster way to make the row: Ctrl + D

Draw one card, press Ctrl + D to duplicate it, and drag the copy to its place. Press Ctrl + D again, and PowerPoint repeats the same distance, so every new copy lands in the right spot. Then align and distribute as above.

Common mistakes

  • Distributing before positioning. If the first and the last shape are in the wrong place, the whole row will be too.
  • Having Align to Slide on by accident. Check the bottom of the Align menu.
  • Selecting only two shapes and expecting Distribute to work. It needs three or more.
  • Aligning by eye. Small gaps are easy to miss on your screen, and show up when the slide is on a big screen.
